abstract: 'This paper proposes a priority-aware unsourced random access (URA) framework to support reliable and efficient data delivery in a massive Internet-of-Things (IoT) environment. Specifically, we develop a cross-layer URA protocol that integrates intelligent preamble selection with effective collision resolution. At the medium access control (MAC) layer, we formulate a weighted throughput maximization problem to prioritize the channel access from event-driven traffic over periodic traffic. A distributed zero-shot Q-learning mechanism is then designed, where machine-type devices (MTDs) select preambles intelligently by leveraging a shared knowledge base, allowing adaptation to network congestion without prior training. At the physical layer, a robust greedy successive interference cancellation (SIC) receiver with reliability masking is introduced to improve multi-user detection and mitigate error propagation during collision resolution. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed protocol significantly improves system performance compared with conventional URA, interleave-division multiple access (IDMA), and on-off division multiple access (ODMA) approaches, in terms of achieving higher throughput, lower per-user probability of error (PUPE), and reduced age of information (AoI).'
